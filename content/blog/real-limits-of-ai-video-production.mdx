A lot gets said about what generative AI in video can do. Less gets said about what it still doesn't do well. After months of producing AI video for brands, here's what we've learned firsthand.

## Character Consistency

Keeping the same face, outfit, or visual style for a single "character" across multiple scenes remains one of the biggest challenges. It requires a clear visual brief and strong reference materials from the start — not just a good prompt. Without that groundwork, characters drift between clips in ways that break the illusion.

## Style Continuity

When you're generating multiple clips for the same project, maintaining a consistent color palette, lighting, and overall look between them takes constant review. A strong initial prompt is not enough on its own. Each clip needs to be checked against the others and corrected where they diverge.

## Complex Movement and Physics

Scenes involving detailed physical interactions — hands handling small objects, for example — still produce errors that a trained eye catches immediately. This is one of the clearest signals that AI video is still catching up to certain categories of content.

## Where It Works Very Well

Conceptual scenes, atmospheric visuals, educational and explanatory content, and producing rapid variations for message testing are all areas where AI video genuinely excels. For these use cases, it's not just "good enough" — it's often the right tool for the job.

## The Real Takeaway

Generative AI in video doesn't replace creative direction or post-production. It makes both possible at a different speed. The final result still depends on someone who knows what to ask for, how to iterate on it, and when to correct it manually.

The technology is powerful, but it's a tool — not an autonomous creative department.
