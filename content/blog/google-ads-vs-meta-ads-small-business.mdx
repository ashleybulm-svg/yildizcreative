If you have a small ad budget, splitting it across every platform at once is the fastest way to learn nothing. The better move is to start where your customers already are — and that depends on one question: **are people already searching for what you sell?**

## The Core Difference

**Google Ads works on intent.** Someone types "dentist near me" or "custom website for restaurant" and your ad appears right when they're looking. You're answering a question they already asked.

**Meta Ads (Facebook and Instagram) works on attention.** People aren't searching — they're scrolling. Your ad has to stop them, make them curious, and create demand that didn't exist five seconds ago.

Neither is better. They do different jobs.

## Start With Google Ads If…

- **People search for your service by name.** Plumbers, clinics, lawyers, movers, repair shops, trucking and logistics — if there's a clear search phrase, Google is where buyers raise their hand.
- **Your customer needs you now.** Urgent or high-intent services convert well on search because the timing is already right.
- **You sell something with a high ticket.** A single client from a well-targeted search can pay for months of ads.
- **You have a website or landing page ready.** Google sends people to a page, and that page has to convince them. Without it, you're paying for clicks that leave.

## Start With Meta Ads If…

- **Your product is visual.** Cosmetics, food, fashion, events, travel, wellness — things people want once they see them.
- **Nobody is searching for it yet.** New products, new concepts, or offers people don't know they need. You have to create the desire first.
- **You want to build an audience, not just close a sale.** Meta is strong for brand awareness, retargeting, and growing followers who buy later.
- **You have good video content.** Short videos are the currency of Instagram and Facebook. A strong 15-second spot will outperform a static image almost every time.

## What a Realistic Starting Budget Looks Like

You don't need thousands to start, but you do need enough to get data. A common starting point for a local business is **$10–$20 USD per day** on one platform for at least **2–4 weeks**. Less than that and the platform's algorithm doesn't have enough results to optimize.

The mistake is spending $5 a day on both Google and Meta and concluding after a week that "ads don't work."

## The Mistakes That Waste the Most Money

1. **No tracking.** If you can't see which ad produced a call, form, or WhatsApp message, you're guessing. Set up conversion tracking before you spend a dollar.
2. **Sending traffic to your homepage.** Ads work best when they land on a page built for that one offer.
3. **Targeting everyone.** "All of Mexico" or "the whole US" burns budget fast. Start with the cities or zip codes where you actually serve clients.
4. **Weak creative on Meta.** On social, the ad *is* the product. A generic stock image won't stop anyone's scroll.
5. **Turning it off too early.** Both platforms need a learning period. Judge results after a few weeks, not a few days.

## When to Use Both

Once one platform is working, the two complement each other well. A common pattern: Meta Ads introduce your brand with video, and Google Ads catch those same people later when they search for you by name. Retargeting — showing ads to people who already visited your site — is often the cheapest win of all.

## The Short Answer

- **People already search for what you do?** Start with Google Ads.
- **Your product sells by being seen?** Start with Meta Ads.
- **Not sure?** Start with the one where you already have the strongest asset: a good landing page favors Google, good video content favors Meta.
