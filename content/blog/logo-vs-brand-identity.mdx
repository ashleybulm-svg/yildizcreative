One of the most common mistakes when starting a business is confusing "having a logo" with "having a brand." They're related — but they're not the same thing. Choosing the wrong one for where your business is right now can cost you time, money, and momentum.

## What a Logo Design Includes

A custom-designed logo with alternate versions and files ready for both print and digital use. It's the central visual piece of your brand — but only one piece.

## What a Full Brand Identity Includes

Brand strategy, color palette, typography, visual direction, and brand guidelines. It doesn't just define how you look — it defines how you're recognized and how you communicate consistency across every touchpoint: social media, website, packaging, advertising, email.

A logo tells people what to call you. A brand identity tells people what to think of you.

## When a Logo Is Enough

If you're just starting out, working with a tight budget, and need something professional to operate immediately — invoices, social media, business cards — a well-designed logo can be the right move for now. It's a foundation you can build on.

## When You Need the Full Identity

If you already have clients but notice your brand "looks different" on every platform — or if you're about to launch a larger campaign, enter a more competitive or premium market, or your competitors consistently look more polished than you — it's time to invest in the full system, not just the symbol.

## The Simple Way to Think About It

A logo is the front door. A full brand identity is the entire building behind it.
